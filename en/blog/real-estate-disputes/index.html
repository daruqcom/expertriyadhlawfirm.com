Real Estate & Rental Disputes in Saudi Arabia

Most real estate and rental disputes in Saudi Arabia center on Ejar registration, non-payment, eviction, or disagreements over a property's condition, and having a properly registered lease is often the single biggest factor in how smoothly a dispute resolves. This guide covers the common scenarios, including developer disputes and title issues.

Why Ejar registration matters so much

Residential and commercial leases in Saudi Arabia generally need registration through the Ejar platform, and an unregistered lease can meaningfully complicate a later dispute or eviction process, since it weakens the documentary basis for either side's position before a court or dispute resolution body.

Registering a lease properly at the outset is one of the simplest things a landlord or tenant can do to avoid a much harder dispute later, and recent regulatory updates have strengthened tenant protections around notice periods and unjustified eviction specifically tied to whether a lease is properly documented.

Common disputes between landlords and tenants

Non-payment of rent, disagreements over the return of a security deposit, disputes over who's responsible for property damage or needed repairs, and eviction disagreements are among the most common issues that arise between landlords and tenants. A landlord generally can't raise rent mid-contract unless the lease specifically allows it, and generally owes written notice of at least 30 days before ending a tenancy.

Many of these disputes turn on what the lease actually says, which is why a clearly drafted, properly registered lease prevents more disputes than it resolves after the fact. Where a tenant believes a property has defects materially affecting habitability, this can in some circumstances support ending the lease without penalty.

Disputes involving developers and off-plan sales

Beyond individual rental disputes, real estate disputes can also involve developers — delayed delivery of an off-plan property sold under the Wafi system, quality issues at handover, or disagreements over a sale's terms — which generally involve larger sums and more complex documentation than a typical rental dispute.

Wafi registration exists specifically to protect off-plan buyers by requiring developer funds to be held in escrow tied to construction progress, and understanding whether a specific project is genuinely Wafi-registered is worth confirming before any significant payment — our off-plan property guide covers this system in depth.

Title deed and land boundary disputes

Title deed and land boundary disputes are a further category, usually requiring careful review of the property's registered records alongside any competing claims, and these can take genuine time to resolve where a property's ownership history involves multiple prior transactions or unclear historical boundaries.

These disputes are exactly why thorough title verification before a purchase — checking for liens, competing claims, and registry accuracy — is worth the time it takes, since resolving a title problem after a purchase closes is considerably more difficult than catching it beforehand.

How rental and property disputes are actually resolved

Many rental disputes are addressed through the Ejar platform's own dispute resolution mechanisms before escalating further, which can be faster than full litigation for straightforward matters. Where a dispute is more complex or involves larger commercial or developer relationships, it typically proceeds through the regular courts or specialized real estate dispute committees.

Having documentation organized — the registered lease, payment records, any written communication about the dispute — before starting formal proceedings meaningfully speeds up whichever path applies to your specific situation.

Direct Answers

Can a landlord evict a tenant without going through Ejar?

An unregistered lease significantly complicates the eviction process and weakens the landlord's position. We can advise on properly registering a lease and on the right process if eviction is needed.

What can I do if my landlord won't return my security deposit?

This is one of the most common rental disputes, and the resolution generally depends on what the lease says and the property's actual condition at handover. We can help pursue what's owed.

Can my landlord raise my rent in the middle of my lease term?

Generally no, unless the lease specifically allows for it — an unauthorized mid-term increase is a legitimate basis for a dispute.

How do I know if an off-plan property I'm considering is genuinely Wafi-registered?

This is worth confirming directly before any significant payment, since Wafi registration is what requires developer funds to be held in escrow tied to construction progress. We can help verify a specific project's actual registration status.

How long do title deed disputes typically take to resolve?

It varies considerably depending on how far back the ownership history needs to be reconstructed — which is exactly why thorough title verification before a purchase is so much more efficient than resolving a dispute afterward.

Penalty for Online Blackmail in Saudi Arabia

Online blackmail and extortion are treated as serious offenses under Saudi Arabia's Anti-Cyber Crime Law, with penalties that can include imprisonment and substantial fines, scaling with the severity and circumstances of the offense. This guide covers what victims and the accused both need to know, including how reporting actually works.

How Saudi law treats this offense

The Anti-Cyber Crime Law covers threats made online to extract money, images, information, or other demands through coercion, and treats this as a serious criminal matter given the harm it causes victims. Penalties can include both imprisonment and significant fines, with more severe consequences where the conduct involves particularly sensitive material, targets a minor, or involves repeated offenses.

Because this is a criminal matter, it's pursued through the Public Prosecution rather than solely as a civil dispute, though a victim may also have grounds for a related civil claim depending on the harm suffered — reputational damage, financial loss, or psychological harm that resulted from the threats.

How to actually report it

Reports can be filed through the Ministry of Interior's dedicated channels, including the 1909 line and the 'Kollona Amn' platform, both built specifically for cybercrime reporting including online blackmail. Filing promptly, with evidence organized, helps investigators move quickly before an account is deleted or evidence disappears.

Many victims hesitate to report out of fear or embarrassment, particularly where the threatened material is genuinely sensitive — but Saudi authorities treat these reports with confidentiality, and the victim is not the one facing legal consequences here.

If you're being blackmailed

Preserve every piece of evidence — messages, screenshots, any payment demands, usernames or phone numbers involved — before it can be deleted or the platform account is closed. Reporting through the proper channel promptly, rather than trying to handle it alone or paying to make it stop, generally produces a better outcome and can prevent the situation from escalating.

This is understandably a difficult and sensitive situation to come forward about, and the firm treats these conversations with complete confidentiality from the first message.

If you're accused of this offense

Where someone is accused of online blackmail, the specific messages and conduct alleged are central to the case, and understanding exactly what's being alleged — and what evidence actually supports it — is the starting point for any defense.

Early legal representation, ideally before or during initial questioning, can materially affect how the matter develops, including whether a matter can potentially be resolved before formal charges are filed in less severe circumstances. Our criminal defense practice handles this from the first consultation.

Related cybercrime offenses worth understanding

Online blackmail often overlaps with related conduct under the same law — unauthorized access to someone's accounts or devices, defamation conducted online, or the non-consensual sharing of private images or information, each carrying its own specific considerations alongside the extortion element.

Where a situation involves several of these elements at once, addressing the full picture rather than just the blackmail component generally produces a stronger case, whether you're the victim or defending against an accusation.

Direct Answers

Can I report online blackmail without revealing my identity to the person threatening me?

There are established channels for reporting these offenses, and we can advise on the right approach for your specific situation and comfort level.

Should I just pay to make the threats stop?

This generally doesn't resolve the situation and often leads to further demands. Preserving evidence and reporting through the proper channel is generally the more effective path — contact the firm to discuss your specific situation.

Where do I actually report online blackmail in Saudi Arabia?

The Ministry of Interior's 1909 line and 'Kollona Amn' platform are both built for cybercrime reporting including this offense. We can also help you prepare and organize evidence before you file.

Will I get in trouble for the material being used to threaten me?

The victim is not the one facing legal consequences in a blackmail situation — the law is specifically built to protect you and prosecute the person making the threats.

What if the person threatening me is anonymous or using a fake account?

Investigators have technical means to trace anonymous accounts in many cases, and preserving all available evidence — even from an anonymous source — is still worth doing immediately.

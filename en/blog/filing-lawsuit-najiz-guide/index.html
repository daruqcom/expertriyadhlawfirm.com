How to File a Lawsuit in Saudi Arabia via Najiz

Filing a lawsuit in Saudi Arabia now runs almost entirely through the Ministry of Justice's Najiz platform, from selecting the correct case type through submission, hearing scheduling, and eventually enforcement if you win. This guide walks through what the process actually involves and where people most often go wrong.

Getting the case classification right from the start

Najiz requires selecting a specific case category and sub-type when filing, a commercial dispute, a labor claim, a personal status matter, and so on each follow different procedural tracks with different required documentation. Choosing the wrong category can mean a case gets rejected or redirected, costing genuine time before it's even properly underway.

This classification step matters more than it might seem, since it determines which court actually hears the matter and what evidence and documentation the system will expect from you at each subsequent stage.

What you actually need before filing

Beyond the statement of claim itself, most filings need supporting documentation specific to the case type, a contract for a commercial dispute, medical records for an injury claim, a marriage certificate for a personal status matter, uploaded directly through the platform rather than submitted physically.

Having this documentation organized and complete before starting the filing considerably speeds up the process, since incomplete submissions typically result in a request for additional documents that adds real delay before the case can actually proceed.

Hearings and how the case actually proceeds

Once filed and accepted, Najiz schedules hearings and notifies both parties electronically, and many procedural steps, submitting responses, requesting extensions, filing additional evidence, now happen through the platform rather than requiring physical court appearances for every step.

Contested cases where the other party disputes the claim generally take meaningfully longer than uncontested matters, and having legal representation from the filing stage, rather than only once a dispute becomes contentious, tends to produce a stronger position throughout.

Enforcement once you have a judgment

Winning a judgment is often not the end of the process, if the other party doesn't voluntarily comply, enforcement runs through a separate execution court process with real tools available: travel bans, salary attachment, and asset freezes among them.

Filing for enforcement promptly once a judgment becomes final, rather than assuming compliance will simply happen, is worth doing given how disruptive these enforcement tools are to a non-compliant party's affairs.

Why 'straightforward' is a judgment call, not a fact

It's tempting to assume your matter is one of the simple, uncontested ones, but whether a case classification is unambiguous, whether the other side will actually contest it, and whether the stakes involved justify the risk of a procedural misstep are judgment calls that are hard to make accurately from the inside. A rejected or misclassified filing costs real time to fix, and that assessment is exactly what's worth getting a second opinion on before filing, not after.

The lawyers help clients from the filing stage through hearings and, where necessary, enforcement, treating the whole process as one continuous matter rather than separate disconnected steps.

Common Questions

Can I file a lawsuit in Saudi Arabia entirely online?

Yes, most case types are now filed through the Najiz platform from submission through much of the hearing process, though some matters may still require physical court appearances at certain stages.

What happens if I select the wrong case category when filing?

This can result in rejection or redirection of the case, costing real time, the lawyers help clients get this classification right from the outset to avoid this delay.

Do I need a lawyer to file through Najiz?

Not always for straightforward, uncontested matters, but legal representation becomes considerably more valuable where the case is likely to be contested or the classification is ambiguous.

What happens if the other party ignores a judgment against them?

This is where the execution court process comes in, with real enforcement tools including travel bans, salary attachment, and asset freezes, the lawyers can help file for enforcement promptly once a judgment is final.

How long does a typical case take from filing to judgment?

It varies considerably based on the case type and whether it's contested. The lawyers will give you a realistic estimate once the lawyers understand your specific situation.

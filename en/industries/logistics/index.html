Logistics — Sector Legal Services

Logistics businesses need counsel on customs compliance, transportation and shipping contracts, and the labor law matters that come with a distributed workforce. Reach the firm on WhatsApp to scope out your options.

Operations combining cross-border and domestic considerations

Logistics operations in Riyadh often combine customs and cross-border considerations with the standard commercial and labor matters of running a large-scale operation, meaning a single logistics business genuinely needs support across several distinct legal areas simultaneously rather than one specialized focus.

We support this range directly, coordinating customs, contract, and labor questions as one coherent picture of a client's operation rather than requiring separate engagements for each dimension.

Customs compliance for cross-border logistics

This falls under our Customs, Import & Export service within Finance, Banking & Tax, and logistics businesses specifically benefit from proactive compliance review given how frequently goods actually move across borders in the course of normal operations.

We help clients build compliance processes that scale with volume, since a customs approach that works for occasional shipments often breaks down under the frequency and scale of genuine logistics operations.

Shipping and transportation contracts

Logistics businesses rely on contracts with shipping lines, trucking partners, and warehousing providers, each with distinct liability and performance considerations, and our Maritime & Shipping Legal Services within Sector-Specific Law covers the maritime-specific elements of this contract portfolio.

Getting liability allocation right across this contract network matters, since a logistics business often sits in the middle of a chain where responsibility for a lost or damaged shipment needs to be clearly traceable back to whoever is actually responsible.

Labor law for a distributed workforce

Our Labor & Employment practice covers this directly, and logistics businesses face particular workforce questions around drivers, warehouse staff, and often a genuinely distributed team working across multiple locations rather than a single centralized office.

This distributed structure raises practical employment questions — from Saudization compliance across multiple work sites to managing termination and end-of-service obligations consistently across a larger, dispersed workforce.

Managing liability across a complex supply chain

When goods are lost, damaged, or delayed somewhere in a logistics chain, establishing where liability actually sits requires careful reconstruction of which party had custody and responsibility at each stage, and we help clients navigate this both as claimants seeking recovery and as parties defending against a claim.

Clear contractual documentation at each handoff point in the chain makes this process considerably more straightforward when a dispute eventually arises.

Technology and platform-based logistics models

Newer logistics businesses built around technology platforms — delivery apps, freight-matching services — face additional questions around worker classification and platform liability that traditional logistics operators may not have encountered, and we help these newer business models navigate genuinely emerging legal questions.

This is an area where we stay current on how Saudi law is actually being applied to platform-based models specifically, rather than assuming traditional logistics precedent applies without modification.

Scaling legal support as an operation grows

A logistics business that started small often outgrows its original informal contract templates and compliance approach well before anyone notices, and we help clients recognize when it's time to formalize processes that worked fine at a smaller scale but create genuine risk at a larger one.

This proactive review is considerably less costly than discovering a gap during an actual dispute or regulatory inquiry.

Education — Sector Legal Services

Educational institutions need counsel on Ministry of Education licensing, ongoing regulatory compliance, and franchise structuring for international education brands. Message the firm on WhatsApp to discuss your institution.

A sector ranging from single schools to global brands

Private education businesses in Riyadh range from single schools to international brand franchises, each with distinct licensing and compliance needs, meaning a founder opening their first school and an international education group entering the Saudi market face genuinely different legal starting points.

We support this full range, tailoring our approach to whether a client is establishing an independent institution or bringing an established international model into the local market.

Ministry of Education licensing

Our Education Law service within Sector-Specific Law covers this directly, from initial licensing application through ongoing compliance once a school or institution is operating, and the specific requirements vary meaningfully depending on the educational level and type of institution involved.

We help founders understand the full licensing pathway early, since discovering a compliance gap after significant investment in facilities and staff is a considerably worse position than confirming requirements before committing capital.

Franchise structuring for international education brands

This combines our Education Law and Franchise practices, and an international education brand entering Saudi Arabia typically needs both sector-specific licensing and a properly structured franchise or licensing agreement governing the relationship with a local operating partner.

Getting this franchise structure right protects both the international brand's curriculum and quality standards and the local operator's investment, and we handle both dimensions as one coordinated engagement.

Ongoing regulatory compliance for operating institutions

Beyond initial licensing, educational institutions face ongoing compliance obligations around curriculum standards, staff qualifications, and facility requirements, and our Public & Regulatory practice supports institutions navigating routine compliance as well as any regulatory inquiry that may arise.

We help institutions build compliance processes that scale as they grow, rather than an ad hoc approach that worked for a small school but creates real risk at a larger scale.

Contracts with parents, staff, and vendors

Educational institutions rely on enrollment agreements with parents, employment contracts with teaching staff, and vendor agreements for everything from facilities to educational materials, and we draft these with attention to the specific liability and performance considerations relevant to an educational operation.

Enrollment agreement terms in particular deserve careful attention, since disputes over fees, withdrawal policies, and service expectations are among the more common matters educational institutions face.

Higher education and specialized institution considerations

Higher education institutions and specialized training providers face additional considerations beyond standard K-12 schooling, including accreditation questions and, for institutions offering internationally recognized qualifications, coordination with foreign accrediting bodies alongside Saudi Ministry requirements.

We help these institutions navigate this dual accreditation landscape, ensuring compliance with both frameworks rather than assuming one satisfies the other.

Disputes involving educational institutions

Where a dispute arises — a fee disagreement with a parent, an employment matter with staff, or a disagreement within a franchise relationship — we handle these with the same care given to the underlying transactional work, since a genuine understanding of an institution's operations meaningfully strengthens dispute resolution.

This continuity between transactional and dispute work means the same team that helped structure your institution understands the context if a disagreement later arises.

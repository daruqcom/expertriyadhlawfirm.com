Family & Personal Status Legal Services

From marriage contracts and divorce to custody and domestic violence protection.

Family & Personal Status covers marriage, divorce, custody, guardianship, and inheritance matters under Saudi Arabia's codified Personal Status Law, handled before the Sharia courts. Personal matters, handled personally

Family law touches some of the most personal moments in a person's life — a marriage, a divorce, a custody arrangement, the disappearance of a family member — and this practice is built around handling each with the discretion and care those moments deserve, not as a routine transaction.

Saudi Arabia's codified Personal Status Law gives these matters a clearer, more predictable legal framework than existed previously, though navigating it still benefits significantly from experienced representation given how much turns on the specific facts of a family's situation.

Marriage, divorce, and the financial questions that follow

Whether it's talaq or khula, a fask dissolution or a paternity matter, or formalizing specific marriage contract conditions before a wedding, each path carries its own procedure and its own financial consequences — and understanding which path actually fits a specific situation, rather than assuming only the most commonly discussed option applies, often changes what someone believed their options were.

These matters rarely stand alone — a divorce typically raises custody and alimony questions at the same time, and we handle these as one coordinated matter rather than requiring separate engagements for what is, in practice, a single family situation.

Custody, visitation, and protecting family members

Custody and alimony arrangements, and making sure a court-ordered visitation schedule is actually honored, are among the most common ongoing needs for families after a separation. Where a relocation, a change in circumstances, or a pattern of denied visitation arises, we help resolve it through the proper legal channel rather than letting either parent act unilaterally.

Where safety is a genuine concern, domestic violence protection is treated as urgent from the first conversation, coordinated where needed with related family matters like divorce or custody, so a person isn't navigating several disconnected legal processes during an already difficult time.

Guardianship and family members who need protection

Guardianship and interdiction formalize the authority to manage a minor's or a vulnerable adult's affairs — a step many families delay until a bank or government office forces the question, when formalizing it proactively is considerably easier.

And where a family member has disappeared or is otherwise unreachable, missing and absent person procedures address both the practical management of their affairs and, where enough time has passed, any broader legal implications, handled with the sensitivity these situations require.

What this practice covers

This includes divorce and khula, custody and alimony, marriage contracts and prenuptial conditions, domestic violence protection, guardianship and interdiction, and the specific legal procedures around a missing or absent family member.

Every matter here is handled with full confidentiality, and the firm is available to discuss sensitive family situations at whatever pace and level of detail feels right for the client.

Are family law consultations confidential?

Yes, completely — attorney-client confidentiality applies fully, and we understand how personal these matters are.

Can this firm handle a family matter involving a foreign spouse or foreign marriage?

Yes — we regularly handle matters with a cross-border element, including questions about which court has jurisdiction and how a foreign marriage or divorce is recognized in Saudi Arabia.

Does the firm handle urgent family matters, like domestic violence situations?

Yes — where safety is a concern, we treat the matter with urgency and can advise on the protective options available.

My situation touches on more than one issue — divorce and custody, for example. Do I need separate lawyers?

No — related family matters are handled as one coordinated case by the same team, since they typically need to be addressed together rather than in isolation.

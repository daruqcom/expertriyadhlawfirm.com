Understanding hidden defects

Saudi civil law recognizes the concept of a hidden defect — a flaw in a purchased item that existed at the time of sale but wasn't apparent or disclosed, giving the buyer grounds to pursue a remedy even after the transaction is complete.

This differs from a straightforward return or exchange situation, since a hidden defect claim addresses a flaw that a reasonable buyer couldn't have identified through normal inspection at the time of purchase.

Pursuing a product liability or hidden defect claim

Where a product has caused injury or property damage, or a purchase has revealed a defect that existed at sale, this practice assesses the specific circumstances to determine the strongest available claim.

Documentation matters significantly here — records of the purchase, the nature of the defect, and when and how it was discovered all support a stronger claim.

Why the discovery timeline matters more than elapsed calendar time

A hidden defect claim generally centers on when the defect was reasonably discoverable, not simply how much time has passed since the original purchase, meaning a defect that only became apparent well after purchase — because it takes time to manifest, or because normal use is what first reveals it — can still support a valid claim even though considerable calendar time has elapsed since the sale itself.

We help buyers understand that hesitating to raise a defect out of uncertainty about whether it's been too long is often based on an inaccurate assumption, since the relevant question is genuinely about discoverability and reasonableness, not a fixed countdown from the purchase date that automatically expires regardless of the actual circumstances.

Why a hidden defect is different from ordinary wear and use

Not every problem that emerges after purchase qualifies as a hidden defect — normal wear and tear, damage from misuse, or a problem that developed after purchase due to circumstances unrelated to the product's original condition are generally treated differently from a flaw that genuinely existed at the time of sale but wasn't apparent, and distinguishing between these categories honestly is an important part of assessing whether a claim is actually viable.

We assess this distinction carefully and honestly before recommending a course of action, since a claim that conflates ordinary wear with a genuine hidden defect is unlikely to succeed, while a claim built around a defect that genuinely existed at sale has real merit worth pursuing properly.

Foreign Investment Lawyers in Riyadh

A MISA license — issued by Saudi Arabia's Ministry of Investment — is the legal authorization that allows a foreign investor or foreign-owned company to operate in the Kingdom, including in most sectors with full 100% foreign ownership. It's required before a foreign-owned entity can obtain its Commercial Registration. The system has changed meaningfully in recent years — the license fee has been suspended under current policy and average processing time has dropped from over a week to roughly two to three business days — but the activity code you register under still functions as a hard legal boundary on what you can actually sell. What a MISA license actually permits

A MISA license is issued under a specific activity category — service, trading, industrial, contracting, real estate, or others — and the category determines which activities the licensed entity can legally carry out, along with any capital or Saudization conditions attached to that category.

Most sectors are now open to full foreign ownership, though a limited negative list of activities still requires a Saudi partner or is restricted outright. Confirming where a planned activity sits on that list is one of the first things worth checking before structuring an investment.

How MISA licensing fits with company formation

The MISA license is obtained before the Commercial Registration, since the Ministry of Commerce requires proof of MISA licensing for any entity with foreign ownership. In practice, the two processes are usually run together so the entity emerges properly licensed at both levels without a gap between them.

Existing MISA-licensed entities also need to keep the license current when they change activity, ownership, or capital — an update that's easy to miss but can affect the validity of the underlying Commercial Registration if left unaddressed.

Why your activity code selection matters more than the fee

With the license fee itself now suspended and processing running in days rather than weeks, the genuine strategic decision at licensing has shifted from cost and speed to scope: the specific activity codes registered on the license operate as a legal perimeter around what the company may sell, which products a bank will book against it, and which sector regulators — telecoms, healthcare, finance, insurance — the entity ends up triggering.

The most common and costly mistake we see is a minimal activity list chosen to get the license issued quickly, which later blocks a natural business expansion and forces a formal amendment rather than a same-day operational decision. We scope the activity list against where the business is realistically headed over its first several years, not just its opening-day operations.

The 2026 shift from licensing to registration

Recent reforms have moved the underlying framework from a mandatory licensing model toward a simplified registration system intended to put foreign and domestic investors on more equal procedural footing, while MISA retains oversight through activity-specific conditions and the negative list for sensitive sectors.

This shift changes some of the practical steps involved but not the underlying analysis a foreign investor needs before committing capital — which activities are open, what capital or partnership conditions attach to them, and how the license interacts with visa sponsorship, banking, and tax registration downstream.

Direct Answers

Which activities are excluded from full foreign ownership?

Saudi Arabia maintains a negative list of restricted activities that require Saudi participation or are closed to foreign investment entirely. We'll check your specific activity against the current list during your consultation.

Does a MISA license expire?

Yes, MISA licenses are issued for a fixed term and require renewal, along with updates whenever the licensed activity, capital, or ownership changes.

Can a MISA-licensed company sponsor foreign employee visas?

Yes — a properly licensed and registered entity can sponsor iqama and work visas for its foreign employees, subject to Saudization (Nitaqat) quota requirements for the company's size and sector.

Is there still a fee to obtain a MISA license?

The license fee has been suspended under current policy, though this is worth reconfirming at the time of application since investment policy in this area has shifted more than once in recent years.

How long does MISA licensing take now?

Processing times have improved substantially and now typically run a few business days for straightforward applications with complete documentation, though complex activity structures can still take longer.

What happens if my MISA activity codes are too narrow for my actual business?

You'll need to file a formal amendment to add activities, which costs time and creates a gap where the expanded activity isn't yet legally covered — better to scope the initial license broadly enough to match your realistic growth plans.

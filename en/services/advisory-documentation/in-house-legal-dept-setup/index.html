In-house Legal Dept Setup in Riyadh

A growing company decides it's time to bring legal in-house and hires its first lawyer based primarily on availability and cost, without first thinking carefully about what the role actually needs to cover — only to find a few months later that the hire is either too junior to handle the company's actual mix of matters independently, or highly capable but sitting underutilized because the role was never properly scoped against the real volume and variety of legal work the company generates. In-house legal department setup helps a growing company design and build its own internal legal function — structure, policies, and initial staffing guidance — rather than relying entirely on external counsel. When a company is ready for an in-house function

As a company grows, the volume and variety of routine legal questions — contract reviews, compliance checks, HR issues — often reach a point where an internal legal function becomes more efficient than routing everything through external counsel.

This doesn't mean external counsel becomes unnecessary — a well-designed in-house function typically works alongside outside counsel for specialized or high-stakes matters, while handling routine work internally.

What this service covers

This includes advising on the right structure and scope for an initial legal function, drafting foundational policies and templates the team will use, and guidance on the profile of legal talent to hire for the company's specific needs.

The goal is a legal function that's genuinely useful from day one, not an underspecified role that ends up needing external help to actually function.

Why the first hire needs to match the actual scope of work

The single most consequential decision in building an in-house function is often the very first hire, and getting the seniority level wrong in either direction creates a real problem — too junior, and the person can't handle the company's actual matters independently and ends up needing constant external support anyway; too senior and specialized for what the role initially requires, and the company overpays for capability that sits underutilized relative to its actual current needs.

We help companies scope this first hire honestly against the real volume and variety of legal work the business currently generates, rather than against an aspirational picture of what the function might eventually need to become once the company has grown considerably further.

Managing the transition from external counsel without losing continuity

Bringing legal work in-house doesn't mean abruptly cutting off the external counsel who has handled the company's matters up to that point — a well-managed transition generally involves the incoming in-house function inheriting institutional knowledge, active matters, and existing templates and precedents gradually, rather than starting entirely from scratch and inevitably repeating work or losing context that already exists.

We help structure this handover deliberately, working with a company's existing external counsel where appropriate to make sure the new in-house function starts with real continuity rather than an information gap that takes months of avoidable rediscovery to close.

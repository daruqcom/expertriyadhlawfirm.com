Legal Translation in Riyadh

A contract is translated by a general translator who handles the language accurately but doesn't fully grasp the specific legal significance of a particular term, subtly shifting the meaning of an obligation or a remedy in a way that goes unnoticed until a dispute arises and both parties discover they've actually been relying on two meaningfully different versions of what they thought was the same agreement. Legal translation covers certified translation between Arabic and English for contracts, court documents, and official filings, where accuracy carries real legal weight. Message the firm on WhatsApp to discuss your translation needs, particularly for a document where precise legal meaning matters as much as general readability.

Why legal translation is different from general translation

A legal document's precise wording often carries specific legal consequences, and a translation that's approximately right isn't good enough when the document will be relied upon in court, before a regulator, or as part of a binding agreement.

Certified legal translation carries a formal weight that a general translation doesn't, and Saudi courts and government bodies generally require this level of translation for official submissions.

What this service covers

This includes translating contracts, court filings and judgments, corporate documents, and official certificates between Arabic and English, prepared to meet the standard required for official use.

Where a document is being translated for a specific purpose — a court filing, an embassy submission, a regulatory filing — the translation is prepared with that specific end use in mind.

Why a legal translator needs to understand law, not just language

A general translator can produce text that reads fluently and accurately in the everyday sense while still subtly misrepresenting a legal term's actual significance, since legal language often carries specific technical meaning that doesn't map directly onto its most natural-sounding equivalent in the other language, and a translator without legal training may not recognize when this distinction actually matters for a given clause.

We use translators who understand the underlying legal concepts, not just the languages involved, specifically because this combination is what actually prevents the kind of subtle, consequential misinterpretation that a purely linguistic translation can introduce without anyone noticing until it's tested in a real dispute.

Why the two language versions need to say the same thing, not just similar things

When a contract or document exists in both Arabic and English, both versions need to reflect genuinely equivalent legal meaning, not simply a fluent-sounding rendering of the general idea, since a Saudi court will typically interpret the Arabic version as controlling and any drift between the two versions can create real ambiguity about what was actually agreed if the parties ever disagree about interpretation.

We review both versions of a translated document together, specifically checking for this kind of drift, rather than treating the translation as a one-directional process where the original document is simply assumed to be correctly and completely represented in the new language.

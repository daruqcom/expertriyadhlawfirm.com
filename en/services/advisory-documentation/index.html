The practical layer behind every legal matter

Almost every legal matter eventually produces a document — a contract, a legal opinion, a notarized power of attorney, a certified translation — and getting these right the first time is often what separates a smooth transaction from a costly delay.

This practice covers that layer directly: legal consultations for general advice, contract drafting and review, formal legal opinions and research, due diligence for transactions, and the notarization, translation, and legal tech services that make everything else enforceable and usable.

Getting advice and building the paper trail

A standalone consultation answers a specific question without requiring a full engagement, while a formal legal opinion documents the legal basis for a significant decision — protecting the people who made it if that decision is ever questioned later by a regulator or an investor.

Before any significant transaction, due diligence verifies what you're actually entering into, and contract drafting and review makes sure the agreement itself actually reflects what both sides believe they're agreeing to.

Making documents official: notarization, translation, and digital platforms

Notarization and attestation through the Ministry of Justice's Najiz-linked system, and certified legal translation between Arabic and English, both need to be right the first time — a document rejected at the moment it's actually needed is far more disruptive than the modest effort of verifying it in advance.

Legal tech and e-POA services round this out, particularly for anyone outside Saudi Arabia needing to authorize a local representative remotely.

Building internal legal capability

As a company grows, in-house legal department setup helps design a structure and initial staffing that genuinely fits the actual volume of legal work, rather than a first hire scoped against an aspirational future need.

Legal training for HR, sales, or procurement teams often pays for itself many times over — a single avoidable legal mistake typically costs far more than the training session that would have prevented it.

Built for both one-off needs and ongoing relationships

Some of what's here is genuinely one-off — a single document that needs notarizing, a contract that needs reviewing before signing. Other parts are built for an ongoing relationship, like setting up an in-house legal department or running legal training for a growing team.

Settlement and sulh services round out the practice, offering businesses and individuals a faster, less adversarial path to resolving a disagreement before it becomes a formal dispute — and we prepare every matter as though headed to litigation even when settlement is the goal, so any resolution reflects genuine leverage rather than eagerness to avoid a fight.

When exactly to reach out

Reach out before signing a significant document, before a major board decision that deserves a documented legal basis, or whenever a document needs to actually work — at a bank, an embassy, or a court — rather than discovering a gap at the moment it's needed most.

Message us on WhatsApp and describe what you need, whether that's a quick question or a more involved document or transaction, and we'll clarify the right service and realistic timeline.

Direct Answers

Do I need an existing legal matter to use these services?

No — a lot of what this practice covers is standalone, like notarizing a single document or getting a contract reviewed before you sign it, with no ongoing case required.

Can this firm help set up a legal function for a growing company?

Yes — in-house legal department setup and legal training are both part of this practice, for companies reaching a size where they need internal legal capability.

What's sulh, and how is it different from mediation?

Sulh is a recognized settlement and reconciliation process under Saudi law, often faster and less formal than mediation, and we can advise on whether it fits your specific disagreement.

Why would we need a formal legal opinion instead of just a consultation?

A formal opinion is a structured written document meant to be relied upon and shared — with a lender, a regulator, or a board — while a consultation is more like an informal conversation. We can advise on which fits your specific need.

Can you help us if a document was rejected for being improperly notarized or translated?

Yes — we can review what went wrong and correct it, though we'd recommend verifying documents in advance next time, since a rejection at the moment a document is actually needed is far more disruptive to fix reactively.

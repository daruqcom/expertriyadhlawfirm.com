Environmental & Water Lawyers in Riyadh

Environmental compliance and permitting

Businesses with an environmental impact — industrial operations, construction projects, and others — need to navigate permitting and compliance requirements under the National Center for Environmental Compliance's regulatory framework.

This includes advising on the permitting process for a specific project or operation, and ongoing compliance obligations once a permit is in place. Energy-sector facilities carry the additional layer covered under energy, oil & gas law.

Water sector regulation and disputes

Water usage, treatment, and related infrastructure fall under specific Saudi regulatory requirements, relevant to businesses in water-intensive industries or those involved in water infrastructure projects.

This practice also represents clients in disputes connected to environmental or water regulatory matters, including enforcement actions and permit-related disagreements. Regulator-issued penalties are challenged through administrative & government law.

Why an existing permit doesn't automatically cover business growth

An environmental permit is generally issued for a specific scope of activity, and expanding operations — increased production volume, a new process, additional facilities — can fall outside what the original permit actually authorized, even where the expansion feels like a natural, incremental extension of an already-permitted business rather than something fundamentally new.

We recommend reviewing environmental permit scope specifically before any meaningful expansion, since assuming an existing permit automatically stretches to cover business growth is a common and genuinely risky assumption that can create real enforcement exposure the business never intended to create simply by growing normally.

Why an Environmental Impact Assessment isn't just a formality for larger projects

Larger industrial and construction projects generally require a formal Environmental Impact Assessment before proceeding, and treating this as a bureaucratic formality to move through quickly rather than a genuine technical and regulatory process can lead to an inadequate assessment that creates compliance problems later, once the project is already underway and much harder to adjust.

We help clients approach the EIA process as a substantive requirement worth getting right the first time, since a properly conducted assessment protects the project's regulatory standing throughout its operational life, while a rushed one can create exactly the kind of gap that surfaces as an enforcement issue well after the project is already built and operating. Construction-phase environmental obligations are coordinated with our construction & engineering practice.

Why water-intensive industries face particular regulatory scrutiny

Given water scarcity's strategic importance in Saudi Arabia, businesses in water-intensive industries — agriculture, certain manufacturing processes, large-scale facilities — often face more detailed regulatory attention around water usage and treatment than businesses with a more modest water footprint.

We help these businesses understand the specific water regulatory requirements applicable to their sector, since assuming standard environmental compliance covers water-specific obligations adequately can leave a genuine gap in an area that regulators treat with particular seriousness given its national strategic significance.

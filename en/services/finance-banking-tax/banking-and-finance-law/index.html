Banking & Finance Lawyers in Riyadh

For lenders: documentation, security, and compliance

Banks and licensed finance companies operating under the Saudi Central Bank's (SAMA) Finance Companies Control Law need loan agreements, security documentation, and credit facility structures that hold up both commercially and under regulatory review.

This includes drafting and negotiating facility agreements, registering security interests, and advising on SAMA's capital, disclosure, and consumer-protection requirements that apply to licensed lenders.

For borrowers: negotiating and enforcing financing terms

Businesses taking on a credit facility need the loan's covenants, security package, and default terms reviewed before signing, since these terms determine what happens if the business later needs more flexibility or falls behind on a payment.

Where a lender moves to enforce security or accelerate a facility, this practice represents the borrower in negotiating a resolution or, where needed, defending the enforcement action.

Why registration is what actually makes security enforceable

A signed pledge agreement over movable assets, inventory, equipment, receivables, creates an obligation between the parties, but it's registration on the unified movable assets registry that actually perfects the security interest and establishes its priority against other creditors with competing claims to the same collateral.

The lawyers check registration status as a routine step in any secured lending matter, on both sides of the transaction, because an unregistered or improperly registered security interest can leave a lender holding what looks like solid collateral on paper but ranks poorly, or not at all, against other creditors when it actually matters.

What SAMA licensing actually requires beyond the loan documents

A SAMA finance company license carries ongoing obligations well beyond the initial approval, minimum capital maintenance, periodic regulatory reporting, and consumer-protection standards that apply to how credit is marketed, disclosed, and collected, obligations that continue for the life of the license, not just at the point of first registration.

The lawyers help licensed lenders build compliance into their loan documentation and collections practices from the start, since a facility agreement that's commercially sound but silent on SAMA's disclosure requirements can create regulatory exposure even when the underlying loan itself performs exactly as expected.

Common Questions

Does a finance company need a SAMA license to lend in Saudi Arabia?

Yes, companies extending credit as a regular business activity generally need a SAMA license under the Finance Companies Control Law, distinct from a one-off related-party loan.

Can loan terms be renegotiated if a business is struggling to pay?

Often, yes: many lenders prefer a renegotiated repayment schedule over enforcement action, and the lawyers can represent a borrower in that negotiation before a dispute escalates.

What security can a lender take over a Saudi company's assets?

Common forms include commercial pledges over movable assets, real estate mortgages, and assignments of receivables, each with specific registration requirements to be enforceable.

Is a signed pledge agreement enough to make our security interest enforceable?

Not on its own: registering the pledge on the unified movable assets registry is what perfects the interest and establishes its priority against other creditors, so registration matters as much as the signature.

What ongoing obligations does a SAMA finance company license carry?

Minimum capital maintenance, periodic regulatory reporting, and consumer-protection standards around how credit is marketed and collected, these continue for the life of the license, not just at initial approval.

If our facility agreement is commercially solid, do we still need to worry about SAMA compliance separately?

Yes, a facility that performs well commercially can still create regulatory exposure if the documentation or collections practices don't meet SAMA's disclosure and consumer-protection standards.

Homicide & Manslaughter Lawyers in Riyadh

A family facing a homicide or manslaughter matter — whether as the accused's family or the victim's — is confronting one of the most difficult experiences a family can go through, often while also trying to understand an unfamiliar legal process during a time of profound grief or fear. Homicide and manslaughter cases involve Saudi Arabia's qisas (retribution) and diya (blood money) framework, among the most serious matters in the criminal justice system, requiring immediate and experienced legal representation. Message the firm on WhatsApp immediately for an urgent, confidential consultation, at any hour, given how time-sensitive these matters are.

Understanding the qisas and diya framework

Saudi criminal law treats homicide and serious bodily harm under a framework rooted in Islamic law that distinguishes between intentional killing, and manslaughter or death caused without intent, with materially different consequences depending on which applies to the facts.

A distinctive feature of this framework is the victim's family's right, in many circumstances, to accept diya (financial compensation) or grant a pardon in place of the maximum penalty — a path that requires careful, experienced negotiation and legal guidance on both sides.

Why experienced representation is essential

These are among the most serious matters a person can face, and building a defense requires a precise understanding of intent, causation, and the specific circumstances of the incident, alongside experienced guidance on the reconciliation process where the victim's family may be open to it.

Given the stakes, the firm treats these matters with the highest priority and will make arrangements to begin work immediately.

The reconciliation process requires genuine skill and sensitivity

Where a victim's family may be open to considering diya or a pardon, the process of reaching that outcome involves far more than a straightforward negotiation over an amount — it requires navigating profound grief, complex family dynamics on both sides, and a process that has to unfold at a pace and in a manner that respects what the victim's family is going through, never rushed or handled without genuine care.

We approach this process with the seriousness and human sensitivity it demands, recognizing that a successful reconciliation depends on far more than legal skill alone — it depends on conducting every conversation with the respect and patience that a family's grief and the gravity of the situation genuinely deserve.

Supporting a family through an exceptionally difficult time

Families on either side of a matter like this are often navigating it for the first and only time in their lives, with little prior understanding of the legal process, the realistic range of outcomes, or how to make sense of what's happening during an intensely difficult period.

We take the time to explain the process clearly and honestly at every stage, help families understand their realistic options, and provide steady, experienced guidance through what is, for almost everyone who faces it, one of the hardest experiences imaginable.

What's the difference between intentional killing and manslaughter under Saudi law?

Intent is central to how these cases are treated, with materially different consequences depending on whether a killing is found to be intentional or the result of an accident or lack of intent. We assess the specific facts carefully.

Can the victim's family agree to a pardon or compensation instead of the maximum penalty?

In many circumstances, yes — the victim's family has a recognized role in this process, including the possibility of accepting diya or granting a pardon. We can advise on and help navigate this process.

Defamation & Slander Lawyers in Riyadh

A false and damaging claim about someone spreads across social media for days before the person it targets even becomes aware it exists, by which point screenshots have circulated widely, the original post may have already been deleted by its author, and the person harmed is left trying to reconstruct evidence of something that's already done real damage to how they're perceived by people who saw it. Defamation and slander cases cover both pursuing a complaint against someone who has damaged your reputation through false statements, and defending against such an accusation. Pursuing a defamation complaint

Saudi law provides recourse where false statements have damaged someone's reputation, whether made in person, in writing, or online, and pursuing a complaint generally starts with documenting the statements and the harm they've caused.

Where the statements were made online or through social media, preserving that evidence promptly — before it can be deleted — meaningfully strengthens the position of anyone pursuing a complaint. Statements published online also fall within the Anti-Cyber Crime Law, covered under our cybercrime practice.

Defending against a defamation accusation

Where someone is accused of defamation, the specific statements at issue and their context matter significantly — a statement of genuine fact, an opinion, or a statement made in a particular legal or professional context can all raise different considerations than an outright false claim.

This practice assesses the specific accusation and evidence to build the right defense for the circumstances. Many defamation matters resolve through the complainant's private right, managed under pardon & dropping rights.

Why speed matters once a false statement starts spreading online

A damaging post can be seen, screenshotted, and shared by others long before the original author takes it down, which means the original post disappearing doesn't actually make the underlying problem go away — the harm and the evidence of it can both persist independently of whether the source material is still visible.

We help people document this kind of situation comprehensively and quickly — the original post if it's still visible, screenshots others may have taken, and the pattern of how it spread — since a delayed response often means working with a fraction of the evidence that existed when the statement first appeared. Reputational and financial harm can also ground a civil claim through our material & moral damages practice.

Understanding qadhf as a distinct and serious category

Qadhf — a false accusation of a serious moral or criminal offense against another person — is treated with particular seriousness under Islamic legal principles reflected in Saudi law, distinct from more general defamatory statements, given the severe and often lasting harm a false accusation of this specific kind causes to the person it targets.

We help clients understand whether their situation falls into this more serious category or a related but distinct form of defamation, since the specific classification affects both the strength of a complaint and the appropriate way to pursue it.

Direct Answers

What counts as defamation under Saudi law?

False statements that damage someone's reputation, made in person, in writing, or online, can give rise to a complaint. We can assess your specific situation.

Can I pursue a complaint over something posted on social media?

Yes — statements made online are treated seriously, and preserving evidence of the post before it's deleted is an important early step.

What if the statement I made was true?

Truth and context matter significantly to how a defamation accusation is assessed. We can review the specific statement and circumstances to advise on your position.

The post about me was deleted before I could screenshot it — is my case over?

Not necessarily — others may have seen or saved it, and we can help you gather whatever evidence remains, including how the statement spread even after the original was removed.

What is qadhf and how is it different from general defamation?

Qadhf refers specifically to a false accusation of a serious moral or criminal offense and is treated with particular seriousness under Islamic legal principles — we can assess whether your situation falls into this category.

How quickly should I act if I discover a damaging false statement about me online?

As quickly as possible — the original post can be deleted or the situation can otherwise change quickly, so prompt documentation preserves the strongest possible evidentiary position.

Legal Memoranda & Pleadings Drafting in Riyadh

A foreign counsel's Arabic-speaking associate translates a well-argued English legal memorandum for a Saudi filing, producing text that is linguistically accurate but structurally unrecognizable as a Saudi court submission — the argument sequence, the citation conventions, and the formal register all still read as an English memo rendered in Arabic words, rather than as a document actually built the way a Saudi judge expects to receive one. Legal memoranda and pleadings drafting is standalone drafting support for the written submissions a Saudi case requires — claims, responses, and legal memoranda — for self-represented parties, foreign counsel, or in-house teams needing precise Arabic legal drafting. Message the firm on WhatsApp to discuss a drafting need, whether that's an original document or a translated one that needs rebuilding to actually work in a Saudi court.

Why the drafting itself matters

Saudi court submissions follow specific conventions around structure, citation of legal basis, and formal register, and a memorandum that doesn't follow these conventions can undercut an otherwise strong argument regardless of how compelling the underlying facts are.

This is particularly relevant for foreign law firms or in-house legal teams who understand their case but need precise, court-ready Arabic drafting rather than a literal translation of English-style legal writing.

How this service is typically used

Some clients need full case representation; others already have their legal strategy set and specifically need a claim, defense, or legal memorandum drafted or refined to Saudi court standards.

This service is available as standalone support, without requiring a broader litigation engagement, for exactly that kind of drafting need.

Why translation and legal drafting are genuinely different skills

A translated legal memo can be linguistically accurate and still fail as a Saudi court submission, because translation preserves the original document's structure and argument sequence, while effective Saudi legal drafting requires building the argument according to conventions a Saudi judge actually expects — how legal basis is cited, how facts are sequenced relative to legal argument, and how formally the document is expected to read.

We draft directly for the Saudi legal audience rather than translating a document written for a different one, which produces a submission that reads as native to the forum it's actually being filed in, not as an import that happens to be in the right language.

Standalone support that fits around your existing team

Foreign firms and in-house teams often already have their legal strategy and factual case fully developed, and what they need isn't a new lawyer taking over the matter, but precise, court-ready drafting support for the specific documents that need to be filed in Saudi Arabia.

We provide exactly this kind of targeted support, working from the strategy and facts you've already developed to produce Saudi-court-ready drafting without duplicating work your team has already done or requiring a broader engagement you don't actually need or want.

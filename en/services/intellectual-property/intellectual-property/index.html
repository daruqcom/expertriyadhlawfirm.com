A founder with a limited budget and a genuinely valuable brand, product design, and some proprietary internal processes all worth protecting doesn't know where to start, and worries about spending scarce resources on the wrong type of protection first — registering something that didn't need it while leaving the thing that actually needed protecting most exposed and vulnerable in the meantime. A starting point for any IP question

Not every IP question fits neatly into trademark, patent, or copyright — sometimes what's needed is a broader conversation about what kind of protection actually fits a specific brand, product, or creative work.

This service exists as the place to start, and the firm will identify the specific type of protection that fits and route the matter accordingly.

General IP strategy

Beyond routing to a specific specialty, this covers general advice on building an IP protection strategy for a growing business — what to register, what to protect contractually, and in what order, given limited time and budget.

This is also the right starting point for a business that's new to IP protection and needs a clear-eyed assessment of where to start.

Why prioritization matters more than trying to protect everything at once

A business with limited resources rarely needs to protect every valuable asset simultaneously and to the same degree — some assets, like a brand name customers already associate with the business, benefit enormously from prompt registration, while others, like an internal process nobody outside the company even knows exists yet, may be adequately protected for now through confidentiality alone without the cost of formal registration.

We help businesses honestly assess what actually needs protecting first, based on real commercial exposure and risk, rather than defaulting to registering everything at once or, just as commonly, protecting nothing formally because the range of options feels overwhelming to sort through alone.

Why the right mix usually combines several types of protection

Most businesses with real IP value end up needing a combination of protections working together — a registered trademark for the brand, copyright covering creative and software output, contractual confidentiality for internal processes — rather than relying on a single type of protection to cover everything the business has built.

We help map out this combination specifically for a business's actual assets and resources, since the right mix genuinely differs from one business to another, and a strategy borrowed wholesale from a different kind of company rarely fits as well as one built around what a specific business actually has to protect.
